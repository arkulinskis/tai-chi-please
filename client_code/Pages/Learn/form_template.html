The lymph, gently

The lymph system is a second circulation: a web of thin vessels just under the skin that carries fluid, waste and immune cells back toward the heart, through hundreds of small filters, the lymph nodes. Unlike blood, it has no pump of its own. It moves with your breathing, your muscles and gentle pressure on the skin. That makes a slow morning of breathing, movement and light massage its natural friend.

The order matters

Clear the drains closest to the heart first, then send fluid toward them, working outward.

Lymph flow, 9 minutes

Four rules

Feather-light

About the weight of a coin. The vessels lie just under the skin: you stretch the skin, you don't knead the muscle.

Toward the nodes

Every stroke moves toward the nearest cleared node, and in the end toward the collarbones.

Slow and rhythmic

About one second to stretch, one to release. Ten times in each place is plenty.

Breathe, then drink

Begin and end with deep belly breaths. A glass of water afterwards is a nice habit.

Check with your doctor first if you have

  • an infection, a fever, or red, hot or painful swelling (such as cellulitis)
  • a blood clot (deep vein thrombosis) or a history of clots
  • heart failure, or serious kidney or liver disease
  • cancer, or you are being treated for it, or you have had lymph nodes removed
  • lymphedema: a certified lymphedema therapist can teach you a routine made for you
  • a pregnancy (skip the belly circles), or recent surgery

Tai Chi Please offers gentle wellness practice, not diagnosis or treatment. Stop if anything hurts, and see a professional for persistent swelling.
